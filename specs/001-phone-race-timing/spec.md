# Feature Specification — Phone-based race timing

**Feature branch** `codex/001-phone-race-timing`

**Created** 2026-11-22

**Status** Draft

JAuswertung uses a Windows desk computer and Android or iOS phones to run heats without paper timing cards. Each heat can use phone timing, handheld stopwatches, or a mix. The first milestone verifies the timing workflow with simulated devices on one computer.

## User Scenarios & Testing

### User Story 1 — Evaluate timing on one computer (P1)

As an organizer, I want to exercise the desk, starter, and lane phones virtually so I can assess readiness before using physical devices.

**Independent test** Run a batch with configurable Wi-Fi jitter, one-way delay imbalance, clock drift, sync interval, and readiness timeout.

1. Given symmetric delays small enough to meet the desk’s bound and zero drift, when the simulator runs a heat, then it reports readiness and zero clock-induced lane error.
2. Given delays that keep the desk’s bound above 10 ms, when the timeout expires, then the heat reports a timeout and the batch counts it separately from ready heats.
3. Given a device with a stale estimate, when the planned finish exceeds its clock-error budget, then the desk withdraws readiness. A fresh qualifying estimate restores it.
4. Given a phone with a valid estimate and fixed clock drift, when it reconnects or its Wi-Fi delays worsen, then it takes fresh sync samples. The desk keeps the prior estimate usable while its bound still covers the expected finish, and shows Resync if that bound expires before a qualifying replacement arrives.

### User Story 2 — Start from the desk or a phone and record lane finishes (P2)

As a starter or lane official, I want to see the selected heat and record the start or the finish for my assigned lanes.

**Independent test** Run one heat with a virtual phone starter and another with the desk as starter. Record finishes on virtual lane phones and check the elapsed times in both cases.

1. Given a ready heat, when the starter taps Start, then the starter sounds the horn and records one start event for the entire heat.
2. Given an official assigned two lanes, when they use one lane’s Finish control, then it records a finish for that lane and the other lane remains available for its own finish. Each control identifies the lane and its swimmer or team.
3. Given a delayed first finish arrives while the attempt is open, the desk uses the original timestamp and records it once for the correct heat and lane. After Apply, a first submission is visibly rejected as closed, and an authorized exact retry of an accepted submission receives the original acknowledgement.
4. Given a ready heat with the desk selected as starter, when the operator selects Start, then the desktop plays the horn through its audio output and records one start event on its own clock. Phone start requests for that heat are rejected.
5. Given a lane already assigned to a phone, when the operator attempts to assign it to a second phone, then the desk shows which phone already has the lane and rejects the conflicting assignment. A finish from a phone without the assignment leaves the lane’s result intact.
6. Given a stale Start screen, an expired grant, or an unreachable desk, when the official tries to start from the phone, then no horn sounds. If playback may have begun and its timely onset cannot be established, the desk shows Start uncertain and the operator must resolve that attempt before a fresh start is enabled.
7. Given the heat panel cannot refresh its snapshot because another operation holds the competition, when the operator views the heat, then the desk marks the prior snapshot stale and disables arming. A successful refresh restores the ability to arm when the heat meets the readiness rules.
8. Given an uncertain start after the grant expires, the operator can confirm the run if they witnessed the horn for that attempt and a valid start capture exists. The desk keeps its finishes and flags the start uncertainty for review. If the operator discards the attempt, a fresh attempt starts with no carried-over submissions.
9. Given a finish captured while Wi-Fi is unavailable, when the official reloads the same browser tab and reconnects during the same desk session, then the app restores the pending finish and retries it with its original timestamp and identity. If the desk permanently rejects it, the app shows the failure to the official.
10. Given the desktop or a phone is selected as starter, when the operator configures the start signal before arming, then the single horn defaults to 0.4 s and the final countdown tone defaults to 0.8 s. Both endpoints of the 0.2–1.5 s range are selectable for either mode. A test tone plays for the selected duration on the starter's audio output and leaves the heat and its timing state intact. A subsequent race start measures every lane from the onset of the horn.
11. Given the desk selects the optional training countdown, when either starter starts the heat, then three short tones sound at one-second intervals before the long horn. All lanes use the onset of the long horn as the start reference. The test tone previews the entire sequence without arming or starting a heat.

### User Story 3 — Review and apply results (P3)

As a desk operator, I want to check captured times against the selected heat before they become competition results.

**Independent test** Review a heat containing a timed lane, a missing time, and a manually entered time. Apply the reviewed values and check the corresponding competition entries.

1. Given a heat with assigned swimmers or teams, when the operator opens its timing results, then each lane shows its assignment and recorded time or missing status.
2. Given a heat with phone-timed lanes and a stopwatch lane, when the starter sounds the common horn, then the stopwatch official can use that signal to start their watch. After the finish, the official can enter the watch’s elapsed time on the phone assigned to their lane, or the desk operator can enter it directly. In either case the desk labels it as manual and lets the operator apply it together with the phone-captured times.
3. Given a stopwatch lane with an assigned phone whose clock estimate has expired, when the official submits a valid elapsed time, then the desk accepts it for review. A submission from a phone assigned only to another lane leaves that result intact.
4. Given background work holds the active competition, when the operator tries to apply reviewed times, then the desk reports that the competition is busy and preserves the review. A later Apply checks the current heat and assignments again before writing any time.

## Requirements

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001** The desk MUST provide the active heat and its lane assignments to connected officials. The mobile pilot MUST protect pairing credentials, assignments, and results in transit with an authenticated encrypted connection. The operator MUST be able to select the desktop or one phone as the sole starter for each heat and assign one or more lanes to each lane phone. Each lane MUST have at most one assigned phone per heat. The desk MUST reject overlapping assignments and identify the phone that already holds the lane.
- **FR-002** Each phone assigned to capture a start or finish timestamp MUST estimate its offset from the same desk clock using four timestamps per sync exchange. The timing system MUST account for round-trip uncertainty, including the possibility of unequal one-way delays.
- **FR-003** Phones assigned to capture start or finish timestamps MUST sync when assigned and refresh in the background. A stopwatch-only phone can pair and submit times without starting clock sync. A valid capture estimate MAY be reused across heats. Schedule the next refresh by the earlier of the configured interval and halfway to the point at which the estimate would no longer qualify through the expected finish. Resample immediately if the selected heat or expected duration makes the estimate insufficient.
- **FR-004** The desk MUST mark a heat ready only when the pairwise clock-error bound for the starter and every phone-timed lane is at most 10 ms through the expected finish. This includes initial sync uncertainty and allowance for drift. A desktop start uses the desk’s reference clock directly, so readiness then depends on the phones recording finish timestamps. For a heat with only stopwatch lanes, a desktop starter needs no clock estimate. A phone starter still needs an estimate with a clock-error bound no greater than 10 ms through the proposed grant deadline. Both modes require a valid heat snapshot and a selected starter. The desk MUST expose missing or expired estimates and timeout status for devices that require sync.
- **FR-011** Before arming, the operator MUST provide a positive expected maximum heat duration. Phone-timed lane readiness MUST cover that duration from the proposed start, and the desk MUST check again immediately before granting a start. For a phone grant, use the latest permitted instant of horn onset as the proposed start. For every phone-captured result, the desk MUST sum the clock-error bounds of the estimates actually used for the start and finish, evaluated at their capture times. A desktop start contributes zero clock-mapping uncertainty. A sum above 10 ms or a finish whose latest plausible desk-clock time exceeds the covered finish MUST be visibly flagged and require explicit approval to apply, even after a mid-heat resync or reload.
- **FR-005** The selected starter MUST record a single heat-start event and sound the horn through its audio output. The desk MUST accept start events only from the selected starter. Lane phones MUST record a separate local monotonic finish timestamp for each lane. The desk MUST map timestamps into its clock reference and subtract the start from each finish.
- **FR-014** Before arming a heat, the operator MUST be able to set the horn duration at the desk for each signal mode. For either starter role, the single horn defaults to 0.4 s and the final countdown tone defaults to 0.8 s. Both support 0.2 s to 1.5 s inclusive. The selected starter MUST offer a separate test tone in that configuration state using the selected duration and its race audio output. Playing a test tone MUST leave the heat state unchanged and create no timing event or start grant. The audible onset of the race horn defines the start reference for all lanes, regardless of the configured duration.
- **FR-015** The desk MUST offer a single horn (default) and a training countdown (“Tut, tut, tut, TUUUUT”) for both starters. The countdown plays short tones at 3, 2 and 1, then the configured horn at 0. Only the onset of that long horn defines the start. The phone MUST request start authorization near the end of the countdown, within the existing authorization window for the long horn. If the final start cannot be authorized in time, it MUST suppress the long horn.
- **FR-012** A phone MUST obtain a one-use, expiring start grant from the desk when the official presses Start. It MUST decline to initiate audio if the conservative upper bound for audible onset falls after the desk deadline. The phone MUST report the start capture separately from the audio outcome. When playback may have begun and timely onset cannot be established, the desk MUST show Start uncertain. After the grant expires, the operator MAY confirm the existing capture if they witnessed the horn for this attempt and its start capture is valid. Confirmation MUST retain its finishes and flag captured results for explicit review approval. The operator MAY instead discard the attempt. Discard MUST close it without applying results, and a new attempt MUST begin without its submissions. The desk MUST block competing starts, assignment changes, and rearming while the grant is outstanding. A delayed or repeated response MUST cause at most one horn.
- **FR-006** Event delivery MUST preserve the original payload, event identity, attempt identity, and capture clock epoch over retry. The phone MUST retain confirmed captures and stopwatch submissions until acknowledged or visibly rejected, including through disconnect, resume, and reload of the same browser tab during the same desk session. The desk MUST accept lane results only from the phone assigned to that heat and lane. A retry of an accepted submission MUST return its existing result, and a conflict MUST leave the first accepted result intact. Successful Apply closes the attempt to new submissions, including first submissions for omitted lanes. The desk MUST reply that the attempt is closed and the phone MUST show that rejection. An authorized exact retry of an accepted submission MUST return its original acknowledgement throughout the same desk session, even after Apply or a new attempt. Stale messages for another attempt MUST leave the active heat’s results intact.
- **FR-007** The desk MUST support stopwatch lanes in the same heat as phone-timed lanes. Stopwatch officials use the same starter horn. The official MUST be able to enter and submit a stopwatch elapsed time on the phone assigned to that lane. The desk operator MUST also be able to enter an elapsed time directly, including for a lane without a phone. Manual entries MUST be validated as elapsed times and MUST be independent of clock-sync readiness. Mobile submissions MUST follow the lane ownership and duplicate handling rules in FR-006. The desk MUST show captured, manual, and missing times and apply reviewed results to the corresponding swimmers or teams through JAuswertung’s heat-based timing workflow.
- **FR-013** Creating or refreshing a live heat snapshot and validating it for arming or starting MUST use the same competition access guard as Apply. If the guard is busy, the panel MUST mark any prior snapshot stale and block arming and new starts until it can refresh and validate the heat. Apply MUST hold exclusive access from snapshot validation through the complete reviewed write. While another operation has conflicting access, the desk MUST reject Apply without changing the competition or discarding the review.
- **FR-008** The simulator MUST run on one computer and report ready rate, timeouts, readiness-time percentiles among ready heats, and lane-time error. It MUST let the operator vary network delays, clock drift, sync interval, and timeout.
- **FR-009** The timing system MUST track clock drift and network delay uncertainty separately. For a phone assigned to capture timestamps, a reconnect or two consecutive failed or non-qualifying refresh exchanges MUST trigger a fresh burst. The desk MAY retain the previous estimate while its sync uncertainty plus drift remains within the lane's error budget through the expected finish. It MUST show Resync when neither the prior estimate nor the new measurements qualify.
- **FR-010** The simulator MUST be able to change Wi-Fi delay and asymmetry during a session and simulate a reconnect independently of the clock drift. Persistent one-way delay imbalance MUST remain part of the uncertainty when judging readiness.

### Key Entities

- **Heat and lane assignment** — The heat, discipline, lane, and assigned individual or team from JAuswertung.
- **Device clock estimate** — The phone’s relationship to the desk clock, when measured, and its uncertainty and drift allowance.
- **Timing event** — A heat start or lane finish with device, heat, local timestamp, and a stable event identity.
- **Reviewed lane result** — The elapsed time or manual value the operator can apply to the competition.

## Success Criteria

- **SC-001** One operator can simulate a starter and at least three lane phones covering four lanes on a single computer. Tests can repeat clean, congested, and asymmetric network scenarios with fixed inputs.
- **SC-002** Every ready heat in deterministic simulator tests keeps each lane’s clock-induced error within ±10 ms under the stated model. A heat with an insufficient clock bound reaches its configured timeout.
- **SC-003** Delaying or retrying a captured timing event leaves its elapsed result unchanged. Applying the reviewed heat writes each time to the correct swimmer or team.
- **SC-004** Before event use, measure clock drift, sync uncertainty, and the time between the recorded start and audible horn with representative Windows, Android, and iOS devices. Record the impact of manual finish reaction time separately when evaluating end-to-end accuracy.
- **SC-005** In a deterministic test, changing the network delay while holding clock drift fixed triggers resampling without increasing the simulated drift. With an expired estimate and persistently poor measurements, the desk shows Resync. When qualifying samples return, the desk restores Ready.
- **SC-006** The simulator can be launched and complete its four-lane validation on one computer before the desktop timing plugin or local device service is available.
- **SC-007** Before the venue pilot, record the deployment’s maximum acceptable wait for Ready and maximum acceptable start timestamp-to-audible-horn error. Before running the pilot, designate at least 30 performance heats per phone platform and a separate fault-recovery suite. The performance set includes scripted reconnect, resume, and same-tab reload scenarios that are expected to complete successfully. It excludes deliberately injected faults whose expected outcome is an aborted, late, or uncertain start. Use an independent common timing reference with a documented error bound. For each platform, use all predesignated performance heats in the Ready-rate denominator, including timeouts. At least 95% MUST become Ready within the declared wait. Every phone-timed lane in each Ready performance heat MUST meet the 10 ms limit including reference uncertainty, and every start exercised in those heats MUST produce exactly one audible horn within the declared error limit. A run MUST stay in its designated set regardless of outcome. Score injected faults separately against their expected rejection, uncertainty, or recovery behavior. Both sets MUST pass. Reconcile every captured submission, including those awaiting acknowledgement. A valid submission interrupted by a transient outage must remain visibly pending until it is delivered once with the original payload after recovery. A deliberately invalid, superseded, or first-time submission for an Applied attempt must be visibly rejected. Any silent loss or duplicate result fails the criterion. A failure attributable to the browser is evidence for evaluating the native follow-up. Network-only failures call for investigating the network and retesting under the same criteria.

## Assumptions and Planning Decisions

- The desk and phones share a local network. The desk provides the common clock reference, so the heat timing workflow can operate without internet time.
- The 10 ms threshold applies to clock-induced elapsed-time error. End-to-end timing acceptance, including the physical horn and manual finish reaction, needs a separate target.
- This feature is intended for manual timing. If one official covers several lanes, closely spaced finishes may be difficult to capture. The desk can review missing or questionable results before applying them.
- The browser pilot tests reload in the same tab during the same desk session. Recovery after closing the tab, clearing browser data, or restarting the desk is outside the pilot scope.
- The existing single-computer simulator assumes zero drift and is the starting point for the first milestone. Published measurements illustrate drift on specific devices. They do not establish the refresh cadence for this deployment.
- The plan will choose native apps or a browser client based on timestamp and horn tests, and determine how to integrate reviewed results into JAuswertung.
